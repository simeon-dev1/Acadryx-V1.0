What Acadryx Actually Does

Not a list of modules. A description of what becomes possible.

Identity & Access

Code login. No emails. No passwords. No IT headaches.

School generates login codes. Parents and students enter them once. Dashboards appear — Admin, Teacher, Student, Parent, Alumni. Each sees what they need. Nothing else.

“A student's account follows them from Pre-KG to Senior Secondary. Never recreated. Never lost.”

  • Five roles, one school — different views, same data
  • Profile permanence across terms, years, sections
  • Alumni keep access after graduation

Academic Boilerplates

Not hardcoded hierarchies. Templates you own.

Start with sensible defaults — Primary, Junior Secondary, Senior Secondary, Arms (A, B, C). Then edit, rename, restructure. It's your school. You decide how it's organized.

“Set your own terms, session dates, result patterns. Change them anytime.”

  • Pre-KG to Senior Secondary — all sections included
  • Rename anything: "House," "Cluster," "Stream"
  • Subject weights for divided teachers (40/60, 30/70)
  • Acadryx remembers your patterns — no rebuilding every term

Results Engine

What took weeks now takes one day.

Teachers enter scores. Acadryx calculates weighted totals automatically. Admin reviews. One click publishes every report card.

“Schools that used paper for 40 years go digital in one term.”

  • Bulk upload via spreadsheet — generate whole classes at once
  • Weighted scoring for subjects with multiple teachers
  • One click publishes every report card in a class
  • Immutable archives — once a term closes, results lock forever

Living Magazine

Your school's story, published daily — not annually.

Events. Student achievements. Photo galleries. News. Admin approves. Teachers submit. Parents see instantly.

“No printing costs. No 50-page PDFs in June for events that happened in December.”

  • Feels like social media — familiar, fast, visual
  • Alumni stay connected to their school's story
  • Annual magazine becomes a highlight reel, not a forgotten archive
  • Prospective parents see a school that's alive, not a brochure
